Ali Oufifi *, Ali Abbas†, Fatem Afghah *, Jonathan Ashton‡, Kurt Türk‡
Modulation recognition is a fundamental task in communication systems. Accurately identifying the modulation method is crucial for achieving reliable signal processing, mitigating interference from coexisting communication technologies, and optimizing networks. Applying deep learning (DL) models to modulation recognition has shown promising prospects in various scenarios. However, traditional deep learning models often perform poorly in online dynamic environments, especially in incremental scenarios - where new modulation methods are encountered during online deployment. Retraining these models using all previously seen modulation methods not only consumes time but may also be impractical due to storage limitations; on the other hand, training solely on new modulation methods often leads to "catastrophic forgetting" of previously learned categories. This issue makes it challenging for deep learning-based modulation recognition models to be applied in real-world scenarios, as the dynamic characteristics of communication systems require the model to effectively adapt to new modulation methods. This paper evaluates the performance of various Incremental Learning (IL) algorithms in dynamic modulation recognition scenarios and compares them with traditional deep learning-based modulation recognition to address these challenges. The research results indicate that an incremental learning-based modulation recognition framework can effectively prevent catastrophic forgetting, enabling robust performance in dynamic scenarios.
Keywords Modulation recognition, Incremental learning, Deep learning, Online learning
1. Introduction
In the field of wireless communication, modulation recognition is of great significance as it facilitates efficient communication, spectrum sharing, and radio frequency fingerprinting. It ensures reliable data transmission in shared environments and supports spectrum allocation by distinguishing unique radio frequency characteristics. Therefore, it becomes an indispensable tool in modern wireless systems. In recent years, deep learning has made significant progress in multiple fields, with its ability to extract effective information from complex datasets being highly influential [1]. This capability has been effectively applied in communication systems, especially in tasks such as modulation recognition and signal recognition. In modulation recognition, deep learning models have shown higher accuracy than traditional methods even in scenarios with a lot of noise, signal fading, and interference [2]. This fully highlights the important value of deep learning in enhancing the modulation detection capabilities of communication systems, regardless of the complex operating environment.
Although deep learning-based technologies have achieved remarkable results in the field of signal recognition, their practical applications in communication systems are still limited due to several key challenges. One major obstacle is that deep learning models lack generalization and adaptability across different scenarios, requiring continuous retraining and optimization to adapt to new tasks or categories. Additionally, obtaining fresh and relevant data increases the complexity of the task, leading to issues with limited computational resources and tight timelines. To fully leverage the potential of deep learning methods and integrate them effectively into communication systems, addressing these challenges is crucial.
Most previous solutions proposed for deep learning-based modulation recognition are based on the "isolated paradigm," where a dataset is first trained on a deep learning model before being used to test the model again. This approach does not focus on retaining acquired knowledge for future learning tasks but only focuses on the final results. The assumptions underlying this paradigm make it applicable only to static tasks in closed environments, which greatly differ from the dynamic characteristics of communication systems, which involve many changing parameters.
This paper focuses on proposing a high-performance deep learning-based modulation detection model to address more realistic dynamic scenarios: as time progresses, the receiver will receive signals using new modulation methods (i.e., new categories). In such scenarios, traditional training methods for deep learning-based modulation detection models have three options: not adjusting the model weights for the new category, only retraining based on the new category, or retraining jointly with all previously seen categories and the new category. The first option can lead to the model being unable to correctly classify the new category, thereby reducing accuracy; the second option can cause "catastrophic forgetting," where the accuracy of the model drops significantly when inputting new information, and the third option is inefficient, requiring both time and resources, along with memory to store all previously seen data, which is unfeasible in some application scenarios and environments. All these choices have shortcomings, making traditional deep learning-based modulation detection models inapplicable to the above scenarios.

Incremental learning (IL) is a machine learning method that aims to continuously update and improve the model's knowledge base and performance by allowing it to interact with new data. Its primary goal is to enable models to adapt to changing environments and evolving datasets without the need for complete retraining, while also retaining previously learned information. To address the issue of memory constraints in highly dynamic communication systems, recent research has applied incremental learning to modulation detection [4]-[6]. Despite the promising prospects shown by incremental learning in modulation detection in these recent studies, there are still many unanswered questions due to the limitations of experimental setups, which will be discussed in detail in the "Related Work" section. These constrained experiments have left a research gap in the field of modulation detection based on incremental learning, and this paper aims to fill this gap.
To this end, this paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the performance of incremental learning-based modulation detection methods: under various continuous dynamic scenarios where new signals using novel modulation techniques are encountered at the receiving end, extensive research has been conducted on multiple incremental learning algorithms based on a large number of modulation schemes. Additionally, this paper conducts memory studies to determine the performance of incremental learning-based modulation recognition models under extremely limited memory budgets.
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: the second part discusses previous research on deep learning-based modulation recognition and incorporating incremental learning into modulation recognition; the third part constructs a dynamic class incremental scenario-based deep learning modulation recognition task model and briefly introduces incremental learning; the fourth part explains the experimental setup and evaluates the results; finally, the fifth part summarizes the entire paper.
II. Relevant Work
In recent years, research on modulation and wireless signal detection using deep learning has attracted significant attention. Various different deep learning architectures have been applied to modulation classification and performance tests, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) [2], [7], Long Short-Term Memory Networks (LSTM) [8], Autoencoders (Autoencoder) [9] etc., all of which achieved competitive accuracy results. Despite the extensive research on modulation recognition based on traditional deep learning models, there are also some studies focusing on proposing non-traditional deep learning frameworks to enhance the adaptability and generalization capabilities of deep learning-based modulation recognition models. In literature [10], researchers proposed a meta-learning framework for modulation classification to improve model generalization, enabling it to adapt to new tasks through a small number of new data samples; in literature [11], researchers proposed a semi-supervised modulation recognition model that is suitable for domain adaptation scenarios with incomplete dataset labels.
In more recent studies, there have been some applications of incremental learning to signal recognition. Literature [4] briefly discussed the task of incremental learning for signal classification: the first task contains five signal types, and the second task contains three new signal types. Literature [12] proposed an incremental learning scheme based on channel separation for Internet of Things device recognition and conducted performance evaluation on broadcast autocorrelation monitoring data. The researchers in literature [13] applied incremental learning to electromagnetic signal classification, adopting an incremental learning algorithm based on category sample selection and multi-objective linear programming classifier. The researchers in literature [5] proposed a complex numerical incremental learning framework for signal recognition and evaluated this framework under the scenario where the signal recognition model encounters a second group of signal types not included in the initial categories. The researchers in literature [6] adopted an incremental learning algorithm based on samples to classify the constellation diagram of eight signal categories.
Despite the valuable outcomes provided by these studies in integrating incremental learning into modulation recognition, there are still shortcomings in the literature. This paper aims to address these deficiencies. Some studies group different modulation methods into distinct tasks within an incremental scenario [4], but this approach does not accurately reflect the actual situation where the receiver's modulation recognition module encounters new modulation methods in a dynamic environment; some studies only consider a limited number of incremental tasks or categories [4], [5] and cannot effectively demonstrate the effectiveness of applying incremental learning to modulation detection; additionally, some papers have only evaluated a limited number of benchmark algorithms and incremental learning algorithms [6].
This paper conducts a comprehensive study on modulation detection methods based on an incremental learning framework. By employing various incremental learning algorithms and testing their effectiveness in a comprehensive evaluation that includes a large number of modulation methods, the paper evaluates under two dynamic scenarios, simulating the receiver's need to respond to new modulation methods in a class-incremental manner. Moreover, memory analysis is conducted to assess the performance of the incremental learning-based modulation recognition model under conditions of highly constrained memory capacity.

Incremental learning (IL), also known as continuous learning or lifelong learning, is a machine learning paradigm aimed at allowing models to continuously update and expand their knowledge base without disrupting existing knowledge. The main goal of incremental learning is to achieve lifelong learning, where the model maintains its proficiency in previously learned tasks while continuously learning new information [14] and [15].

All these assumptions and challenges essentially require an effective and adaptive incremental learning model to carefully balance two fundamental characteristics: plasticity and stability. Plasticity refers to the ability of the incremental learning model to quickly adapt to new information or data points. A model with strong plasticity can quickly absorb new knowledge and adjust its parameters to accommodate changing data distributions or new tasks. On the other hand, stability refers to the ability of the incremental learning model to retain and consolidate existing knowledge. A model with strong stability should avoid forgetting or overwriting existing information when encountering new data.
In the field of incremental learning, researchers have proposed various strategies to mitigate the problem of catastrophic forgetting. Regularization methods employ various regularization techniques to constrain the parameters of the model during its learning of new tasks, thereby reducing interference with existing knowledge; parameter isolation techniques isolate and protect specific model components related to previous learned tasks, allowing for the integration of new knowledge while preserving existing knowledge; additionally, replay-based incremental learning stores and periodically replays samples from previous tasks to maintain their impact on the model, preventing performance degradation. These samples are commonly referred to as "sample paradigms," and in scenarios where memory budgets are limited, they can be selected through random or predefined strategies to better represent the corresponding task / category.
In the context of class-incremental learning with a single incremental head, models tend to favor the most recent classes. However, in task-incremental learning with multi-head models, this bias is mitigated as each task has its own head, and parameter updates for new tasks do not affect the heads of other tasks. In the aforementioned incremental learning methods, the replay-based approach can leverage sample examples to better counteract the biases that arise from class-incremental learning with a single incremental head. To this end, this paper introduces three state-of-the-art replay-based incremental learning methods, which will be briefly introduced below:
1  
iCaRL[15]: This method proposes to save memory buffers based on the similarity between data and category prototypes, and conduct knowledge distillation between old models and new models to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
2  
BiC[14]: In addition to randomly selecting sample examples to replay the previous task, BiC also introduces two new parameters to eliminate the bias of unified classifiers. These two new parameters can greatly help the incremental learner reduce the error prediction for new categories.
3  
LUCIR[16]: This method normalizes the feature values before the final fully connected layer. Additionally, it sets a boundary in the embedding space for each category to enhance the linear separability of the features, thereby helping the model avoid interference with the previously learned embedding spaces. The memory buffer in LUCIR is constructed through a random selection approach.

In the experiment, this study adopts the iCaRL, BiC and LUCIR as the modulation recognition model based on incremental learning. In addition, in order to better evaluate the performance of modulation recognition based on the incremental learning framework, two non-incremental learning benchmark models are also adopted in the experiment, which are detailed as follows:
•  
Traditional Deep Learning Model: At each time stage, the backbone model updates its parameters by training on the dataset available at that stage.
•  
Joint Learning: At each time stage, the backbone model is trained using a dataset of all observed modulation schemes. Joint learning essentially represents an ideal scenario —— there are no retraining or memory constraints because it assumes that at any given time, all previously seen modulation scheme datasets can be obtained for retraining. Consequently, this benchmark model sets an upper limit on the achievable accuracy for other methods.
(II) Accuracy Experimental Figure 3 presents the accuracy of the modulation recognition models based on incremental learning and the benchmark model under the "adding 2 categories every time" scenario, both with high signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and low SNR. In this experiment, all three incremental learning modulation recognition models used 2000 sample examples. From the figure, it is evident that traditional modulation recognition models suffer from catastrophic forgetting, failing to retain information learned from previously observed categories. On the other hand, the performance of the incremental learning models (especially the BiC model) closely approaches the joint learning baseline model as the accuracy upper limit. This indicates that through the incremental learning framework, we can effectively adapt to new categories without storing all previous datasets while retaining the information learned from previous observations.
Figure 3 includes two subfigures: (a) "Adding 2 categories every time" scenario with an SNR = 0 dB, and (b) "Adding 2 categories every time" scenario with an SNR = 20 dB, both using a horizontal axis for category count (from 2 to 24), and a vertical axis for accuracy. The curves in the figures represent the trend in accuracy for traditional deep learning models, iCaRL, BiC, LUCIR, and the joint learning model, respectively.
To further investigate the performance of modulation recognition models based on incremental learning, this study also considered the scenario where "4 new categories are added each time," which consists of a total of 6 consecutive tasks. Figure 4 shows the accuracy of various comparison models under the "4 new categories added each time" scenario. It is evident from the graph that traditional deep learning models suffer from catastrophic forgetting. For modulation recognition models based on incremental learning, the BiC model effectively avoids catastrophic forgetting, especially when the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is 0 dB, with the accuracy of the BiC model approaching the ideal joint learning accuracy. Under the SNR = 0 dB condition, compared to traditional deep learning models and joint learning models, both iCaRL and LUCIR models also demonstrated good performance. However, under the SNR = 20 dB condition, the performance of the LUCIR model decreased compared to other incremental learning models. It is important to note that in this study's dataset, for each modulation method and at each SNR level, there were 4096 samples. This means at the final stage of this scenario, the joint learning model could obtain a total of 2000×24 = 48000 samples; whereas all incremental learning modulation recognition models only stored 2000 samples, yet their performance approached that of the joint learning model (especially the BiC model). This result fully demonstrates the effectiveness and efficiency of these models in dynamic modulation recognition.

Conclusion: This paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the incremental learning framework in wireless signal modulation recognition under dynamic online scenarios (i.e., the receiver will encounter new modulation methods over time). The study results indicate that traditional deep learning-based modulation recognition models cannot retain the information of previously observed modulation methods. In contrast, the incremental learning-based modulation recognition models can both retain existing information and adapt to new modulation methods. Among various algorithms used for incremental learning-based modulation recognition, the BiC algorithm performs best, achieving near-ideal accuracy at a signal-to-noise ratio of 0 dB. Moreover, in another test experiment to evaluate the effectiveness of the incremental learning-based modulation recognition model under varying memory sizes, we found that even with extremely limited memory (only storing 200 samples), applying the incremental learning framework to modulation recognition can significantly improve the recognition accuracy in dynamic scenarios.
